## What this decision does, and the one thing it does not

```text
SELECTED        the governance METHOD for reaching a P13 definition
NOT SUPPLIED    the CONTENT of that definition — GAP-0001 stays open
NOT AUTHORIZED  drafting, until FD-P13-002 resolves the scope question
NOT AUTHORIZED  construction · canonicalization · certification · Native Core #12
```

**The selection of Option B is not permission to begin drafting.** The record
says so three times, in `§6`, `§12` and its own decision body: *"No P13
Blueprint drafting action that depends on this unresolved scope question may be
treated as authorized merely from selection of Option B."* Its
machine-readable summary reads `NON_CANONICAL_DRAFT: PERMITTED_ONLY_SUBJECT_TO_FD-P13-002`
and `DRAFTING_AUTHORITY: SUBJECT_TO_FD-P13-002`.

**Claude has therefore not begun drafting.** The scope question this decision
defers to `FD-P13-002` is the same one `ACT-CC-P13-003 O1` escalated rather than
resolved by acting: `DEL-T4.4-CF-001`'s scope is bounded to the *AIOS
Construction Phase*, and P13 is not an authorized construction phase. Option B
being the recommended path — and now the chosen one — changes nothing about that
factor. Acting on it now would be inferring authority from the Founder's
agreement with a recommendation, which is neither a grant nor a scope ruling.

## An internal inconsistency in the record, and the rule that resolves it

The record's metadata block reads `Status: PENDING FOUNDER DECISION`, and `§14`
describes what holds *"until Founder explicitly selects an option"*. Its
decision field at `§15` is nonetheless completed: Option B marked, rationale
given, signed *Moriarty*, dated 18 September 2026, `Decision Status: FINAL —
ISSUED`, with a Founder Final Attestation beneath it.

**The decision body governs, by the record's own rule.** `§16` states that the
record *"shall be interpreted strictly according to its actual decision body."*
The `PENDING` line is the template's pre-issuance value carried through
unamended; the decision body is the act. This reading is recorded here rather
than applied silently, because reading a `PENDING` status as `ISSUED` is exactly
the kind of inference that must never be made quietly. **If the Founder intended
the record to remain pending, this note is the place to say so and the selection
below should be treated as not yet made.**

11. RECOMMENDED PATH FOR FOUNDER CONSIDERATION

Recommended: OPTION B — NON-CANONICAL DRAFT → FOUNDER CANONICALIZATION.

This recommendation is an architectural/governance recommendation, not an authority claim.

The rationale is:

1. P13 identity is currently not established.
    A direct Founder definition can solve this, but it places the entire synthesis burden on Founder before the available evidence has been fully transformed into a coherent proposal.
2. Claude already has delegated architecture and engineering capabilities.
    The Delegation Charter explicitly permits architecture discovery, architecture design, architecture decisions, decomposition, dependency/interface analysis, and supporting documentation within delegated scope.
3. The critical authority boundary can remain intact.
    Claude may prepare the technical synthesis without becoming the canonical authority.
4. The distinction between draft and canon is exceptionally important here.
    P13 is precisely the situation where prematurely treating an architecture proposal as canonical could create a self-reinforcing governance error.
5. Founder retains the final constitutional/canonical decision.
    The Founder remains the terminus of the §58 canonicalization mechanism.
6. The process produces better evidence for the Founder decision.
    Instead of asking Founder to define P13 in isolation, Founder receives a reconciled proposal grounded in actual AIOS state.
7. Path B does not weaken Founder authority.
    It actually makes the boundary explicit:

Claude = synthesis / draft authority
Founder = canonicalization authority

8. The remaining scope uncertainty is visible rather than hidden.
    FD-P13-002 can explicitly resolve whether the drafting activity falls within the delegated scope.

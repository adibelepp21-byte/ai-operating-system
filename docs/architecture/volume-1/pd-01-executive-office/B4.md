1. Purpose

B4 defines the Team architecture that implements the capabilities owned by the Executive Sub Divisions.

B4 answers:

“How are the capabilities owned by each Executive Sub Division realized through Teams?”

The intended architecture is:

Strategic Mandate
        ↓
Capability
        ↓
Executive Sub Division
        ↓
Team
        ↓
Role Group

Team design is capability-driven, not person-driven.

⸻

2. Team Design Principles

TA-01 — Capability-Driven Team Design

Teams are created to implement organizational capabilities.

They are not created merely around individuals or job titles.

⸻

TA-02 — One Team, One Primary Purpose

Each Team should have one primary purpose.

A Team should not be designed as an uncontrolled container for unrelated domains.

⸻

TA-03 — Operational Responsibility Without Authority Conflict

Teams may execute responsibilities associated with their capability.

They do not independently create authority outside the Authority Model.

⸻

TA-04 — Minimum Necessary Structure

Teams should be sufficient to implement the required capability without creating unnecessary fragmentation.

⸻

TA-05 — Team-to-Capability Traceability

Every Team must trace to an owned capability.

No Team should exist without a defined capability relationship.

⸻

6. Team Boundary Rules

Rule 01 — Team Does Not Redefine Authority

A Team operates within authority delegated through the organizational and governance model.

⸻

Rule 02 — Team Does Not Become Platform Owner

A Team under PD-01 does not automatically become the technical or operational owner of another Platform.

⸻

Rule 03 — Team Purpose Must Be Traceable

Every Team must trace to:

Team
  ↓
Capability
  ↓
ESD
  ↓
PD-01 Mandate

⸻

Rule 04 — Team Separation of Concern

Distinct responsibilities remain separate where combining them would create:

* conflicting accountability;
* unclear ownership;
* authority ambiguity;
* uncontrolled scope.

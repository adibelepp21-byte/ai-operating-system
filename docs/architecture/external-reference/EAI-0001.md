## 6. Architecture Observations

**[A]** unless marked otherwise.

- **O-1 — Quality is treated as a direction, not a state.** jcode does not
  assert that swallowed-error patterns are absent; it asserts the count does not
  grow (E-7). This is a conformance posture AIOS does not currently express.
- **O-2 — The ratchet's unit is a count, not an identity.** `total = 3243` and
  per-file counts (E-9) locate violations to file granularity at best. A
  violation can be moved or replaced without detection so long as the count
  holds.
- **O-3 — Baseline expansion is self-service.** `--update` (E-8) lets the same
  actor who introduced a regression legitimize it. The mechanism's integrity
  rests on review convention, not on structure.
- **O-4 — The dependency guard is default-open.** An internal crate absent from
  both lists warns and passes (E-5). Enforcement covers a named denylist, not
  the space of possible dependencies.
- **O-5 — Boundary rationale is compile-time, not architectural.** E-2 states
  the goal plainly: recompilation surface. Architectural cleanliness is
  described as instrumental to it, not as the objective.
- **O-6 — Risk classification is explicitly asymmetric.** E-10 reasons from
  consequence magnitude rather than probability, and resolves ambiguity by
  escalating. The gate is a *decision path* reached by automation.
- **O-7 — Coordination constrains shape, not topology.** E-11 bounds report size
  and requires a TL;DR. Nothing in it restricts which agent may message which.

## 8. Evaluation

### EP-1 — Ratcheting Quality Budgets

**[A] AIOS has a real expressive gap.** AIOS conformance can state *zero* or
*one frozen exception* and nothing between. P7-F-2's five `KnowledgeError` sites
are recorded as a single `expectedFailure`; nothing structurally prevents a
sixth from being absorbed into the same marker unnoticed. jcode expresses the
missing middle — but through a mechanism (O-2, O-3) whose weaknesses AIOS must
not inherit.

**Compatibility:** the *principle* is compatible; the *mechanism* is not.
Direct adoption would import a count-based, self-service, default-tolerant
control into a fail-closed architecture.

### EP-2 — Type-Crate / Behavior Separation with Automated Guard

**[A] ALREADY PRESENT, and stronger in AIOS.** AIOS enforces the same
separation with a fail-closed allowlist at import-statement granularity across
eleven conformance suites, versus a default-open denylist of 22 names at
workspace-manifest granularity (E-4, E-5). Adopting the external form would be
a strict weakening. Its independent arrival at the same principle is
corroborating evidence and is retained as such.

### EP-3 — Graduated Risk Gate with Asymmetric-Cost Reasoning

**[E] Compatibility: UNVERIFIED.** AIOS Tool execution semantics are not built;
there is no execution path for a risk gate to sit in front of, so no comparison
can be completed on evidence.

**[A]** A graduated gate is a decision path reached by automation (O-6). Placing
one in AIOS would put automation in a position to decide, which PR-3 (Detect
Don't Decide) and Constitution §6.2 invariant 2 reserve. That tension is real
but not adjudicable until the Tool seam exists.

### EP-4 — Swarm Coordination via Structured Message Contracts

**[A] Structurally inapplicable.** jcode constrains message shape while leaving
topology open (O-7). INV-13 makes the topology itself unrepresentable — AIOS has
no peer channel to put a contract on. The message-shape discipline is sound
engineering and is retained as knowledge; it provides no argument for relaxing
INV-13.

### 9.1 Adopt

None.

### 9.2 Adapt

**EP-1**, accepted **only** in its adapted AIOS form — the **Bounded Exception
Register** — never as a copy of jcode's ratchet. The adaptation shall preserve
fail-closed enforcement, explicit exception identity, append-only governance,
Architect authorization, traceability to a Finding or Governance Decision, and
**no self-service baseline expansion**.

It may record known exceptions, prevent new ones, identify existing exception
locations, require explicit governance authorization for any increase, and
permit the set to shrink through normal maintenance. It must **not** become a
mechanism for silently normalizing architectural violations.

**[A]** The decisive design difference from EP-1's source: the AIOS form is an
**allowlist of identified sites**, failing on any *new* site and on any *absent*
site, rather than a count that merely may not grow (O-2). Growth is an Architect
act recorded in the Governance Decision Register — never a `--fix` or `--update`
flag (O-3).

### 9.3 Reject

**EP-2** and **EP-4**. Rejection means: evaluated, and not accepted into the
AIOS architecture under the current baseline. **REJECT ≠ irrelevant.** The
evidence and reference knowledge below remain available to future review.

### 9.4 Observe

**EP-3**. Monitored knowledge. No AIOS architecture is modified, no Tool
boundary redesign is authorized, and no risk classifier or decision gate is to
be implemented. A future EAI review may revisit this reference when AIOS Tool
execution semantics become sufficiently mature.

## 12. Reference Knowledge

Retained regardless of decision outcome.

| ID | Knowledge |
|---|---|
| **RK-1** | A conformance regime can express *direction* as well as *state*. "Not worse than the recorded baseline" is a governable position between "clean" and "broken" — provided the baseline's unit is an **identity**, not a count. |
| **RK-2** | A quality budget whose unit is a count cannot distinguish a violation that moved from one that was fixed (O-2). Identity-based baselines are strictly more informative at the same enforcement cost. |
| **RK-3** | Any mechanism carrying a self-service escape hatch (E-8) transfers its integrity from structure to convention. Where governance authority is the thing being protected, the escape hatch must be an authority act, not a flag. |
| **RK-4** | Default-open enforcement (E-5) covers the cases someone thought of. Default-closed enforcement covers the cases nobody thought of. The difference is invisible while the enumerated set stays current, and decisive the moment it does not. |
| **RK-5** | Separating data contracts from runtime behavior is arrived at independently by systems under compile-time pressure (E-2) and by systems under architectural-integrity pressure. Convergence from unrelated motivations is corroborating evidence for the principle. |
| **RK-6** | Asymmetric-cost reasoning (E-10) — sizing a control by the magnitude of its worst failure rather than by its probability — is a transferable analytic frame, independent of whether the specific gate is ever adopted. |
| **RK-7** | Constraining message *shape* and constraining message *topology* are different controls answering different risks (O-7). A system that has structurally eliminated a topology has no need of the shape contract that governs it. |

## 13. What This Reference Means for AIOS Architecture

1. **What did the external system do?** Enforced seven CI-wired guardrails
   (E-12) over an 83-crate workspace, combining ratcheting budgets that permit
   bounded existing debt (E-7) with a denylist dependency guard (E-4), an
   asymmetric-cost risk classifier for destructive shell commands (E-10), and
   shape-constrained agent reports (E-11).

2. **Why does it matter?** It demonstrates a conformance posture AIOS cannot
   currently express — bounded, directional tolerance — and it independently
   corroborates two principles AIOS already holds more strictly.

3. **What is different in AIOS?** AIOS is fail-closed where jcode is
   default-open (E-5); identity-based where jcode is count-based (E-9);
   governance-authorized where jcode is self-service (E-8); and structurally
   constrained by INV-12 and INV-13 where jcode constrains by convention.

4. **What, if anything, should AIOS change?** **One thing, in principle only:**
   adopt bounded, identity-based exception tracking in the adapted **Bounded
   Exception Register** form (§9.2). Nothing else. Three of four patterns
   produce no AIOS change at all, and the one approved pattern is approved as a
   *direction*, not as work.

## Context

`ADR-0010` established **Platform Division** as the canonical organizational-unit
entity, with **Department** as its recorded historical alias, and applied a
deliberately bounded amendment to five locations in the Canonical Domain Model.

`ACT-CC-F03-011` (Phase D independent verification) found that the bound was set
too narrowly. Finding **F-D1** recorded that 20 non-alias `Department`
occurrences survive inside the Domain Model, including:

- **INV-10** — `Cross-Department Capability dependencies` — an invariant of the
  same class as the amended INV-1 and INV-2;
- the canonical **ownership table** (`Capability | Exactly one Department`),
  which restates INV-1's rule in the superseded vocabulary;
- the canonical **ownership and governance relationships** (`Organization owns
  Department`, `Department owns Capability`, `Department owns Agent Definition`);
- the entity-table rows for **Capability** and **Agent Definition**, which define
  those entities in terms of `Department`.

---

## Problem Statement

The decisive question is what kind of artifact the Canonical Domain Model is.

It declares `**Status:** Canonical` and `**Authority:** AIOS Semantic
Foundation`. **It is a living canonical artifact, not a historical record.**
Every statement in its body is a current assertion about current architecture.

ADR-0010's alias rule permits `Department` to remain where it is *historical
evidence*. Inside a living canonical artifact, **none of the 20 occurrences
qualifies**: they are not records of what was once true, they are statements of
what is true now. The alias rule was therefore being asked to reconcile a
document with itself, which is not what an alias rule is for.

The result was a canonical model that stated the same invariant twice in two
vocabularies — INV-1 in the new term, the ownership table in the old — and an
invariant set split between both.

---

## Decision

**Within the Canonical Domain Model, every current canonical reference to the
organizational unit uses `Platform Division`.**

**The historical-alias rule is retained, and its scope is restated:** it governs
`Department` as it appears in **evidence outside this document** — historical
Acts, prior Governance Decision Register entries, findings, review reports,
ADR-0001 … ADR-0009, the Volume 1 and Volume 2 corpora, and commit history. Those
remain untouched and remain readable through the alias.

`INV-10`'s `Cross-Department` becomes **`Cross-Platform-Division`**. The
hyphenation binds the compound so it reads as *across Platform Divisions*, which
is the sense the PD-02 corpus already carries for this concept (`Cross-Platform`
appears 208 times across 41 bodies, including `C8 — Cross-Platform Architecture
Governance`).

## Non-Scope

Unchanged, and verified unchanged:

- **All historical evidence** — historical Acts, prior GDR entries, Finding
  Register, Appointment Register, commit history.
- **ADR-0001 … ADR-0009**, and **ADR-0010** itself.
- **The Volume 1 corpus** and **the Volume 2 / PD-02 corpus** — the Volume 2
  bodies are byte-identical to Founder-supplied source and are never rewritten
  for terminology.
- **The Engineering Constitution**, the **Architecture Freeze**, `native_core/`,
  and the engineering specs.
- **No repository-wide search-and-replace.** The amendment is confined to one
  file.

---

## Why this is not the global migration GDR-0020 §3 excludes

`GDR-0020 §3` recorded that the FD-6 *decision* did not authorize renaming every
occurrence of `Department`, global search-and-replace, or alteration of
historical evidence. That constraint is respected in full: no historical evidence
is altered and no repository-wide replacement occurs.

What this ADR does is different in kind — it completes the reconciliation of a
**single living canonical artifact** with the decision that already governs it,
under the Founder's explicit remediation directive that the canonical model must
not be left internally contradictory.

---

## Alternatives Considered

| Alternative | Why rejected |
|---|---|
| Leave the 20 occurrences, rely on the alias rule | The alias rule governs historical evidence; it cannot make a living document consistent with itself. Leaves INV-1/INV-2 and INV-10 in different vocabularies and the ownership table restating INV-1 in the superseded term |
| Add a cross-reference at INV-10 only | Addresses the single most visible symptom; leaves the ownership table, relationships, and entity rows contradictory |
| Rewrite the Volume 2 corpus for uniformity | Prohibited — the bodies are byte-identical to Founder-supplied source |
| Repository-wide replacement | Prohibited by `GDR-0020 §3`; destroys historical readability |

---

## Consequences

**Positive.** The Canonical Domain Model is internally consistent in one
vocabulary. The invariant set is uniform. The ownership table and INV-1 no longer
state the same rule in two terms. Historical evidence is fully preserved and
remains readable through a rule whose scope is now stated accurately.

**Accepted cost.** The Domain Model's hash changes a second time. Documents
outside it continue to use `Department`; that is intended, and it is now
correctly described as historical usage rather than as an unexplained remainder.

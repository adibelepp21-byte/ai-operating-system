# ADR-0008

## Metadata

- **Identifier:** ADR-0008
- **Title:** Establish the Engineering Department and its first two Capabilities, Engineering Intelligence and Cognitive Intelligence
- **Status:** Approved

This document records a proposed architectural-tier decision under
Engineering Constitution §3.4. It describes a change to Department and
Capability structure. It grants and defines no authority of its own.

## Context

### The governance state this decision enters

Phase 5 (Intelligence Ecosystem) implementation is authorized. Its
measurable Exit Criteria are ratified and fixed as E5-1 through E5-5,
recorded at `docs/governance/AIOS_GOVERNANCE_DECISION_REGISTER_v1.0.md`
(GDR-0005). Three of those criteria bear directly on this ADR:

- **E5-1** — *"For each in-scope Intelligence category, at least one (1)
  capability is implemented and verified. In-scope category count = 2.
  Minimum verified capabilities = 2."*
- **E5-2** — the Cognitive Intelligence first milestone: *"a single unit
  of execution work is decomposed into two (2) or more ordered sub-steps,
  demonstrated on real execution rather than plan."*
- **E5-3** — the Engineering Intelligence first milestone: *"exactly two
  (2) of the seven sub-capabilities … **Coding** and **Testing** — are
  implemented and verified. The remaining five (Architecture, Security,
  Review, Refactoring, Documentation) are **not** required for Phase 5
  exit."*

E5-1 requires capabilities to be *implemented*. Constitution §3.3 places
implementation-tier work *"within already-approved Capabilities,
Architecture Decision Records, and principles."* Constitution §15 states
that an architecture artifact — *"a new Department or Capability"* — is
done only when *"approved at the Architectural Tier through an
Architecture Decision Record,"* and that *"No tier's completion criteria
substitute for another's."* Canonical Domain Model §6 states that
Department and Capability are *"Created/retired via architectural
decision, architect approval."*

Taken together: the two Capabilities E5-1 counts must exist as approved
architecture before implementation against them is in-tier. No such
Capability exists today. This ADR is the instrument that closes that gap.

### The structural state this decision enters

One Department exists — **Platform** — owning one Capability, Governance
Artifact Integrity, both created by ADR-0003 and recorded per the
Organization Framework at `docs/architecture/organization/platform/`.
ADR-0003 states that Platform *"is not a catch-all for future business or
product Departments' work."* Its Consequences record that it *"establishes
the first worked template for future Department and Capability creation
decisions under Constitution §3.4's Department-and-Capability-structure
category."* This ADR is the second exercise of that template.

Architecture Freeze v1.0 §13 names **Phase 5 — Department Architecture**
[A] as the phase in which *"Organization/Department/Capability ownership
[is] realized as a governed structure."* Native Core Blueprint §28 places
the same expansion under governed phases, not ad-hoc growth. This ADR
performs exactly that realization at the Spine level, and nothing below it.

### The engineering state this decision enters

The Native Core `core/capability/` boundary exists and enforces the
Capability model structurally: single-Department ownership (INV-1),
explicit versioned dependencies (INV-9), governance-recorded
cross-Department dependencies (INV-10), a queryable and observable
dependency graph (INV-11), and zero-implementer flagging (INV-14).
Roadmap §9.6 built that boundary against a *governance stub* for
Department ownership, explicitly *"Blocked by [O]: Department ownership
(Phase 5) for full realization."*

This distinction is load-bearing and is preserved here:

- Building the **Capability boundary** is a projection of the Canonical
  Domain Model (§8: later artifacts are *"projections of this model, not
  extensions to it"*) and required no ADR.
- Creating **Capability instances** changes *Department and Capability
  structure*, which Constitution §3.4 reserves to an ADR.

This ADR does the second only. It creates no code, modifies no boundary,
and changes nothing in `core/capability/`.

## Problem Statement

Phase 5's ratified Exit Criteria require at least one implemented and
verified capability for each of two in-scope Intelligence categories —
Cognitive and Engineering. Neither category has an approved Capability to
implement against, and no Department exists that is accountable for
either.

Platform cannot absorb them: ADR-0003 scoped Platform to AIOS's own
governance and semantic substrate and expressly excluded catch-all use.
Assigning AIOS's engineering and reasoning work to Platform would blur the
accountability boundary ADR-0003 drew, which Constitution §9 forbids
absent an ADR amending the Canonical Domain Model directly — an amendment
this situation does not warrant and this ADR does not propose.

Without an architectural-tier decision, Phase 5 implementation would
either proceed against Capabilities that do not exist — outside
Constitution §3.3's tier boundary — or stall.

## Decision

Create, under Constitution §3.4's *Department and Capability structure*
category:

1. A Department named **Engineering**.
2. A Capability named **Engineering Intelligence**, owned by Engineering.
3. A Capability named **Cognitive Intelligence**, owned by Engineering.

All three are recorded per the ratified Organization Framework convention
at:

- `docs/architecture/organization/engineering/README.md`
- `docs/architecture/organization/engineering/capabilities/engineering-intelligence.md`
- `docs/architecture/organization/engineering/capabilities/cognitive-intelligence.md`

Creating those records is the implementation of this Decision and occurs
only after this ADR is approved. No such file exists at the time of
proposal.

**Explicit confirmation of scope: no Canonical Domain Model amendment
occurs.** Zero entities, relationships, ownership rules, lifecycle rules,
or invariants are added, removed, or altered. This Decision instantiates
already-ratified entity types; it does not amend their definitions. No
Canonical Domain Model text is touched.

**Explicit confirmation: Platform remains unchanged.** The Platform
Department, the Governance Artifact Integrity Capability, and their
recorded definitions, scope, and ownership are untouched by this ADR.
Nothing is transferred out of Platform and nothing is added to it.

**Explicit confirmation: this ADR follows ADR-0003; it does not supersede
it.** ADR-0003 remains Approved and fully in force. See Contradiction
Review, below.

### 1. Department Definition — Engineering

**Name.** Engineering.

**Slug.** `engineering` (Organization Framework, Naming Convention).

**Purpose.** Engineering is the Department accountable for AIOS's own
construction and self-directed reasoning: the abilities by which AIOS
builds, changes, verifies, and plans work on itself.

**Authority boundary.** Engineering holds no Constitutional Tier
authority, no independent Canonical Domain Model approval authority, and
no authority to create or retire Departments or Capabilities. It owns
Capabilities and Agent Definitions and is accountable for them, per
Canonical Domain Model §2 and §5, and nothing further.

**Relationship with Platform.** Engineering is a peer of Platform, not a
subordinate and not a successor. Platform remains accountable for AIOS's
governance and semantic substrate — the Constitution, the Canonical Domain
Model, and their subordinate procedural documents. Engineering is
accountable for building and reasoning about AIOS. Neither Department
absorbs, supervises, or overrides the other. No ownership moves between
them, in either direction, by this ADR.

**Relationship with its Capabilities.** Engineering owns exactly the two
Capabilities named in this Decision. Ownership is accountability, not
implementation: per Canonical Domain Model §2, a Capability is a contract
independent of what implements it.

**Admission rule (binding on future ADRs).** A Capability enters
Engineering only when its accountability is AIOS's own construction or
self-directed reasoning, and only by a further Architecture Decision
Record. **Being intelligence-related is not sufficient.** Engineering is
not a generic container for intelligence work. A Capability whose
accountability lies in a business, product, scientific, quantitative,
linguistic, creative, or strategic domain does not enter Engineering
merely because it involves reasoning or models; it requires its own
Department decision. This rule is stated at creation time, deliberately,
so that the boundary is fixed before the pressure to widen it exists.

### 2. Capability Definition — Engineering Intelligence

**Name.** Engineering Intelligence.

**Owner.** Engineering Department (exactly one — INV-1).

**Slug.** `engineering-intelligence`.

**Definition.** The ability to construct, change, verify, and safeguard
AIOS's own engineered artifacts, as a governed contract independent of the
models, tools, or techniques used to deliver it.

**Full capability scope (long-term architecture).** Seven sub-abilities:

1. Architecture
2. Coding
3. Testing
4. Security
5. Review
6. Refactoring
7. Documentation

**Phase 5 realization (intentionally limited).** Only two of the seven —
**Coding** and **Testing** — are realized in Phase 5. The remaining five
(Architecture, Security, Review, Refactoring, Documentation) are **not**
required for Phase 5 exit, per ratified criterion E5-3.

**The Capability boundary is long-term architecture; the Phase 5
realization is a deliberate, bounded subset of it.** The two are not the
same and neither redefines the other. Declaring the full seven-part scope
now does not authorize building the other five, and building only two now
does not narrow the Capability's contract. This follows Constitution §8 —
complexity is added only when the problem requires it — while preserving
Constitution §1.3's principle that Capabilities persist independently of
what currently implements them.

**Non-scope.** This Capability does not cover governance-artifact
maintenance, which belongs to Platform under ADR-0003. It names no
technology, language, framework, model, vendor, or infrastructure; per
Canonical Domain Model §8, only Runtime and Tool may do so.

### 3. Capability Definition — Cognitive Intelligence

**Name.** Cognitive Intelligence.

**Owner.** Engineering Department (exactly one — INV-1).

**Slug.** `cognitive-intelligence`.

**Definition.** The ability to reason about, decompose, sequence, and
review work before and after it is performed, as a governed contract
independent of the models or techniques used to deliver it.

**Full capability scope (long-term architecture).** Three sub-abilities:

1. Reasoning
2. Planning
3. Reflection

**Phase 5 realization (intentionally limited).** Only two elements are
realized in Phase 5:

- task decomposition
- ordered planning

These correspond to ratified criterion E5-2: *"a single unit of execution
work is decomposed into two (2) or more ordered sub-steps, demonstrated on
real execution rather than plan."* Reasoning and Reflection, beyond what
decomposition and ordering require, are **not** realized in Phase 5.

**As with Engineering Intelligence, the Capability boundary is long-term
architecture and the Phase 5 realization is a deliberate, bounded subset
of it.**

**Non-scope.** This Capability does not execute anything. Per
capability_spec §5 and §8 and Architecture Freeze §4, a Capability
*"must not execute itself."* It names no technology, language, framework,
model, vendor, or infrastructure.

### 4. Explicit transient invariant state (INV-14)

Immediately following this ADR's approval, both Engineering Intelligence
and Cognitive Intelligence will have **zero Agent Definitions implementing
them**. This is precisely the condition Canonical Domain Model §7
invariant 14 identifies: *"A Capability with zero active Agent Definitions
implementing it is an invalid steady state and must be flagged for
governance review."*

This is an expected, temporary condition and is acknowledged here
explicitly rather than discovered later. It is not an oversight. This ADR
deliberately creates no Agent Definition: per Canonical Domain Model §6,
Agent Definitions are *"created/deprecated at Department discretion within
Capability governance"* — Implementation Tier work under Constitution
§3.3, not architectural-tier work requiring an ADR. Future implementation
work may create Agent Definitions implementing these Capabilities, as
ordinary follow-up work outside this ADR's scope. This ADR creates no
Agent Definition and constrains no Agent Definition structure: it states
nothing about how many Agent Definitions exist, or about which
Capabilities any one of them implements.

This treatment follows ADR-0003's explicit transient invariant state
handling exactly. It is a governance flag, not a governance breach:
`core/capability/` reports the condition through `orphan_capabilities()`
and takes no action on it, consistent with Detect Don't Decide (PR-3).

## Alternatives Considered

1. **Assign both Capabilities to the existing Platform Department.**
   Rejected. ADR-0003 scopes Platform to AIOS's governance and semantic
   substrate and states it *"is not a catch-all for future business or
   product Departments' work."* Building and reasoning about AIOS is not
   governance-artifact maintenance. Adopting this option would blur an
   accountability boundary an Approved ADR drew, which Constitution §9
   forbids without an ADR amending the Canonical Domain Model directly.
   It would also leave the two Capabilities' true accountability
   unresolved, deferring rather than answering the question.

2. **Create a new Engineering Department owning both Capabilities.**
   **Selected.** It resolves accountability at the point the question
   arises, keeps Platform's ADR-0003 scope intact, and matches the Spine
   shape the Canonical Domain Model prescribes: Department owns
   Capability, each Capability owned by exactly one Department. It
   requires the admission rule stated in the Decision to prevent the new
   Department becoming the generic container this ADR exists to avoid.

3. **Split ownership — one Capability to Platform, one to a new
   Department.** Rejected. Engineering Intelligence and Cognitive
   Intelligence may come to depend on one another as Phase 5 proceeds.
   That is an architectural possibility weighed here, not an established
   fact and not a decision — **no dependency is introduced by this ADR.**
   Should such a dependency arise, splitting the two across Departments
   would make it a cross-Department dependency, requiring a governance
   record under INV-10 for what would, in substance, be work internal to
   one accountability boundary. That would be governance overhead created
   by an ownership choice rather than by a real boundary.

4. **Create the Department now and defer both Capabilities to later
   ADRs.** Rejected. It leaves an inert Department owning nothing —
   ADR-0003 rejected the same shape for the same reason — and would not
   satisfy E5-1, which counts implemented capabilities, not Departments.

5. **Split this into three separate ADRs (one Department, two
   Capabilities).** Rejected. The three elements are one decision: neither
   Capability can exist without the Department, and the Department's
   scope is defined by exactly which Capabilities it owns. Separating them
   would produce ADRs that cannot be evaluated independently and would
   create a window in which an approved Department owns nothing. This
   disposition follows the Founder determination recorded under Q2 of the
   P5-S5 authorization: a single ADR, no splitting.

### What becomes possible on approval

AIOS's Spine gains a second accountability boundary and its first
Capabilities oriented at AIOS's own construction and reasoning.
Implementation work toward E5-2 and E5-3 becomes Implementation Tier work
under Constitution §3.3, because it would then be *"within already-approved
Capabilities."* Until approval, it is not.

At the architectural level, the Spine gains a second Department, so INV-1
and INV-10 describe a genuine multi-Department structure rather than
holding over a single Department alone. This is a change to the governed
model, recorded in documentation only. This ADR wires no implementation:
it modifies no source file, no test, and no runtime behaviour, and
`core/capability/` is unchanged by it.

### What is deliberately left undone

No Agent Definition, Skill, Workflow, or Tool is created. No Phase 5
implementation is performed. Five of Engineering Intelligence's seven
sub-abilities and the un-decomposed portions of Cognitive Intelligence are
left unrealized by design, per E5-2 and E5-3.

**ADR-0003 in detail — followed, not superseded.** Four reasons:

1. **Different subjects.** ADR-0003 creates Platform and Governance
   Artifact Integrity. This ADR creates Engineering, Engineering
   Intelligence, and Cognitive Intelligence. No name, definition, scope,
   or ownership statement is shared or contested.
2. **Nothing is reversed or reinterpreted.** Under the ADR Framework, an
   ADR is required to *"reverse or materially reinterpret a prior ADR."*
   This ADR does neither. Every statement in ADR-0003 remains true and
   operative after this ADR's approval.
3. **ADR-0003's exclusion is honored, not overridden.** ADR-0003 states
   Platform *"is not a catch-all for future business or product
   Departments' work."* This ADR acts on that exclusion by creating a
   separate accountable Department rather than widening Platform, and
   carries the same discipline forward through Engineering's own
   admission rule.
4. **ADR-0003 invited this.** Its Consequences record that it *"establishes
   the first worked template for future Department and Capability creation
   decisions under Constitution §3.4's Department-and-Capability-structure
   category."* This ADR follows that template: same structure, same
   Organization Framework compliance check, same explicit transient
   INV-14 acknowledgment, same explicit confirmation of zero Domain Model
   change.

**One tension addressed explicitly.** ADR-0003's Alternatives Considered
rejected *"Create multiple Capabilities at once"* on the ground that it
*"risks under-considered scope for any individual Capability."* This ADR
creates two Capabilities at once. That is not a contradiction, for two
reasons. First, an ADR's Alternatives section records options weighed for
that decision; ADR-0003's binding content is its Decision, which contains
no rule limiting future ADRs to one Capability. Second, the risk ADR-0003
named does not obtain here: both Capabilities' Phase 5 scope was fixed in
advance by ratified criteria E5-2 and E5-3, and neither is being scoped
speculatively at drafting time. The disposition follows the Founder
determination recorded under Q2 of the P5-S5 authorization.
